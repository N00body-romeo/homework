HMWK: Welford’s recurrences and sampling statistics

Exercise 1 derives the one-pass update for the sum of squares and the variance. Exercise 2 runs it on m hackers attacking n systems and compares the results with μ and σ².

Exercise 1: Welford’s recurrence formulas

Take scores x1, …, xk. Write Mk = (x1 + … + xk)/k for the mean, SSk = Σ (xi − Mk)² for the sum of squares, and sk² = SSk/(k − 1) for the sample variance (k ≥ 2).

Knuth’s recurrence for the mean

From k·Mk = (k − 1)·Mk−1 + xk:

Mk = Mk−1 + (xk − Mk−1) / k   (A)

Equivalently, xk − Mk−1 = k·(Mk − Mk−1)  (B).

The same approach for SS

Expand the square: SSk = Σ xi² − k·Mk². Subtracting the same identity at k − 1 leaves only the last term of the first sum:

  1. SSk − SSk−1 = xk² − k·Mk² + (k − 1)·Mk−1²
  2. Since (k − 1)·Mk−1 = k·Mk − xk, we get (k − 1)·Mk−1² = k·Mk·Mk−1 − xk·Mk−1
  3. Substituting and grouping: SSk − SSk−1 = xk·(xk − Mk−1) − k·Mk·(Mk − Mk−1)
  4. By (B), k·(Mk − Mk−1) = xk − Mk−1, so the difference is (xk − Mk)·(xk − Mk−1)
SSk = SSk−1 + (xk − Mk−1)(xk − Mk) = SSk−1 + (k − 1)/k · (xk − Mk−1)²

The second form uses xk − Mk = (k − 1)/k · (xk − Mk−1), which follows from (A). Start from M0 = 0 and SS0 = 0.

Variance

Divide by k − 1 (or by k for the population variance):

sk² = SSk/(k − 1) = (k − 2)/(k − 1) · sk−1² + (xk − Mk−1)² / k   (k ≥ 2)

One pass, constant memory, and no subtraction of two huge nearly equal numbers (the weakness of Σx² − k·M²).

Conclusions


Averages against theory. For every n, the average of the sampling means sits on μ, because the sample mean is an unbiased estimator: E[x̄] = μ. The average of the sampling variances sits on σ² when the denominator is n − 1: E[s²] = σ². With denominator n the average is σ²·(n − 1)/n, which is clearly too low for small n and disappears as n grows.


Why this matters in inference. In practice there is one trajectory and μ, σ² are unknown. Unbiasedness says x̄ and s² are not systematically off, so they are legitimate stand-ins for μ and σ². The spread of x̄ across hackers is its sampling distribution, and its standard deviation σ/√n is the standard error, which is what confidence intervals and tests are built on (with the CLT supplying the shape). Welford’s recurrences give both estimates in a single streaming pass.


Effect of n. The averages do not move, since they were already centred on μ and σ². What shrinks is the variability. The standard deviation of the sampling means follows σ/√n: quadrupling n halves it. The variance of s² is (μ4 − (n − 3)/(n − 1)·σ⁴)/n, also of order 1/n, so the two lines in the chart are parallel with slope −1/2 on log-log axes. The level of the s² line depends on the fourth moment of the distribution. As n grows, x̄ → μ and s² → σ² for each hacker (consistency, the law of large numbers).
