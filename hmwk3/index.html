Reading the table

Variance cells show simulated value, then theory. Var(Sₙ) = nσ² grows, Var(Sₙ/n) = σ²/n vanishes, and Var(Sₙ/√n) = σ² stays fixed, which is why √n is the scale of the fluctuations.

The last column is the Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance between the standardized scores (Sₙ − nμ)/(σ√n) and the standard normal CDF Φ.

What n and m do to the aggregate score

Adding up the scores of all m attackers gives the aggregate T = Σⱼ Sₙ⁽ʲ⁾, a sum of mn i.i.d. increments.

  • System size n sets the scale of a single attacker. The mean score nμ grows linearly, the spread σ√n only like a square root, so Sₙ/n concentrates around μ (LLN) while Sₙ/√n keeps a constant spread σ.
  • Number of attackers m does not change the law of one attacker. It sets how sharply the histogram resolves that law, with sampling noise of order 1/√m, and it scales the aggregate: mean mnμ, standard deviation σ√(mn).
  • Rademacher case. Each step gains or loses a point with equal chance, so μ = 0 and the aggregate is a pure random walk with no drift. Sₙ/√n approaches N(0, 1) and the aggregate wanders on the scale √(mn).
  • Bernoulli case. Sₙ counts the compromised systems, so μ = p > 0 and the aggregate drifts upward. Its relative fluctuation σ/(μ√(mn)) shrinks as either n or m grows. Sₙ/√n is centred at μ√n and diverges, so only (Sₙ − nμ)/√n converges to a normal.
