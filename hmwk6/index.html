Theoretical Background

Concept of Independence

Two categorical variables X and{' '} Y are{' '} statistically independent when knowing the category of{' '} X provides no information about the distribution of{' '} Y:

P(X = x, Y = y) = P(X = x) · P(Y = y)

Equivalently, the conditional row proportions{' '} P(Y = y | X = x) remain identical across every row.

Pearson's Chi-Square Test (χ²) & Cramér's V

We compare Observed counts (O) against{' '} Expected counts (E = Row Total × Col Total / N) under independence:

χ² = Σ [ (O − E)² / E ] ,   df = (R − 1)(C − 1)

Because χ² scales linearly with sample size{' '} N, Cramér's V normalizes the statistic to{' '} [0, 1] to measure association strength.

Association vs. Causation & Sample Size Rules

A significant χ² test establishes statistical association, not causation (confounding variables may explain the pattern). Additionally, the asymptotic χ² approximation requires at least 80% of cells to have E ≥ 5 and no cell with E < 1.
